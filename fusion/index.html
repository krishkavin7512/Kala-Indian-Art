Saṅgam

where two rivers of painting meet

Two rivers, one confluence.

One tradition draws the world with a few white lines on a mud wall. The other floods cotton with plant dyes in dozens of patient stages. Saṅgam — Sanskrit for the meeting of rivers — asks what happens when they flow together.

वारली

Warli

The Warli people of the Sahyadri foothills paint their lives — dances, harvests, weddings, forests — in a grammar of just three shapes.

Region
Palghar, Dahanu, Talasari and Jawhar, north Maharashtra
Makers
The Warli Adivasi community; traditionally women painted for weddings, and Jivya Soma Mashe took the art onto paper in the 1970s
Surface
Hut walls plastered with mud, cow-dung and red ochre (geru)
Medium
Rice paste mixed with water and gum, applied with a chewed bamboo stick
Shapes
Circle (sun, moon), triangle (mountains, trees, the human body), square (the sacred chauk)

కలంకారీ

Kalamkari

'Pen-work' in Persian: cotton drawn with a bamboo kalam and dyed with plants and minerals — from temple epics to the Tree of Life palampores loved across the world.

Region
Srikalahasti (freehand pen style) and Machilipatnam–Pedana (block-printed), Andhra Pradesh
Makers
Hereditary artisan families; Coromandel-coast workshops exported painted cottons to Europe and Southeast Asia
Surface
Cotton cloth soaked in myrobalan and buffalo milk
Medium
Mordants (alum, iron), madder / chay root, indigo, myrobalan — up to 17 stages of dyeing and washing
Motifs
Tree of Life, lotus, peacock, paisley (mango), epic narratives and deities

The artwork · 2026

The Tree of Life and the Tarpa Dance

An original digital painting, drawn entirely in code as layered vector art. A Kalamkari Tree of Life rises from the centre of a Warli tarpa dance, under a Warli sun whose rays open like Kalamkari petals, inside a palampore frame whose inner band is a Warli chain of dancers.

Hover over any element to learn which tradition it comes from. Isolate each tradition, or explode the painting into its five layers.

WarliKalamkariFusion

How we fused them.

A fusion is only convincing if it has rules. We gave each tradition the job it does best, then let them trade motifs where they already share a meaning.

01

Warli draws the living

Every human and animal is built from Warli's circle and two triangles, drawn in rice-white — dancers, farmers, deer and bullocks.

Warli grammar
02

Kalamkari grows the plants

Trees, flowers, leaves and the frame use the kalam's flowing line and the natural-dye palette: madder, indigo, myrobalan yellow, iron-black.

Kalamkari line & dye
03

Find the shared symbol

Both traditions picture life as a cycle — the Warli tarpa spiral and the Kalamkari Tree of Life. So the tree grows from the centre of the dance.

Common meaning
04

Cross-pollinate

Warli sun rays become Kalamkari petals; dancers wear madder and indigo patterns; the tree bears Warli circle-fruit; a Warli frieze runs inside the border.

Fusion motifs
05

Compose as a palampore

The layout borrows the palampore: an arched field, indigo spandrels and a flowering border — filled with the everyday world of a Warli village.

About this project

Exploring similarity, difference and creative synthesis.

Purpose
To explore the similarities and differences between regional painting styles and encourage creative synthesis, by creating an artwork that combines elements of two styles.
Styles fused
Warli (Maharashtra) — pure geometry in rice-white — with the intricate, dyed line of Kalamkari (Andhra Pradesh).
The artwork
“The Tree of Life and the Tarpa Dance” (2026), an original digital painting drawn as layered SVG vector art with custom code, so it can be deconstructed layer by layer and printed at any size.
Materials
Digital painting tools: hand-written SVG and JavaScript (with GSAP for motion). Reference photographs from Wikimedia Commons, credited on the credits page.
